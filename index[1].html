Bought once. Loved since.

A book's second reader knows things the first one didn't.

Every copy on our shelves has a past owner. Some left a name on the flyleaf, some left a receipt from a shop that doesn't exist anymore, some left a whole conversation in the margins. We don't erase any of it — we catalog it.

We buy in small batches from estate sales, library de-accessions, and the occasional trunk of a retired professor. Nothing arrives pristine, and that's the point.
